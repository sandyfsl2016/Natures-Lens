Submission Checklist

  • To get an idea and determine whether your paper is appropriate for this publication, read the Aims & Scope.
  • Utilize the LaTeX or Microsoft Word templates to get your manuscript ready.
  • Verify that the following matters have been properly taken into account: authorship, copyright, publication ethics, research ethics, figure forms, data, and references format.
  • Make sure each author has read the Instructions for Authors and has given their approval to the submitted manuscript's content.
  • The authors are urged to publish their work on SciProfiles and include a biography (optional) with their submission.

Manuscript Submission Overview

Types of Publications

There are no limitations on the maximum length of papers in Nature Lens: The Forensic Science journals as long as the material is thorough and succinct. To replicate the results, complete experimental details must be supplied. As per the requirements on Supplementary Materials and references to unpublished data, writers are required by Forensic Sciences to publish all experimental controls and, whenever feasible, make complete datasets accessible.

Submissions to the Nature Lens: The Forensic Science journal should not be previously published or being considered for publication in another journal. The following are the primary categories of articles:

  • Articles: All original manuscripts for research. All original research articles are accepted by the journal as long as they include studies that are solid from a scientific standpoint and offer a significant amount of novel information. Although brief communications with important but preliminary findings will be taken into consideration, authors should avoid needlessly splitting their work into multiple linked papers. Peer review will take the study's impact and quality into account.
  • Reviews: These offer succinct and accurate summaries of the most recent developments in a particular field of study. The PRISMA guidelines ought to be adhered to by systematic reviews.
  • Case Report: In order to improve the report, detailed information of the rarest of the information and results are all covered in depth in case reports. Every case report must include a statement of impact and relevance, as well as a review of the literature that demonstrates how the present instance or series of cases advances our understanding of the subject.

Accepted File Formats

It is recommended that authors create their manuscripts using either the LaTeX or Microsoft Word templates. The time needed to finish copyediting and publish accepted papers will be significantly reduced by using the template file. All files must have a combined data size of no more than 120 MB. Please get in touch with the editorial office at email …………. if this is an issue. File formats that are accepted are:

  • Microsoft Word: Before being submitted, manuscripts created in Microsoft Word must be combined into a single file. The Nature Lens: The Forensic Science Microsoft Word template file is what we recommend using when writing manuscripts in Microsoft Word. After the first citation paragraph, please include your graphics (schemes, figures, etc.) to the main body of the text. Before submitting, the authors' names and any associated metadata should be eliminated.
  • LaTeX: All LaTeX-prepared manuscripts must be compiled into a single ZIP archive, complete with all source files and images, so that the supplied PDF can be recompiled by the Editorial Office. We recommend using the Nature Lens: The Forensic Science LaTeX template files while writing LaTeX publications. Additionally, you can now submit manuscripts straight to Forensic Sciences via the online tool writeLaTeX. From the writeLaTeX template collection, choose the Nature Lens: The Forensic Science LaTeX template file.
  • Supplementary files: Any format is acceptable; however, if feasible, it is advised to utilize widely used, non-proprietary formats (for more information, see below).

Disclaimer

Free Format Submission

Nature Lens: The Forensic Science accepts free format submission:

  • Although we don't have any strict formatting guidelines, all manuscripts must include the following sections: Funding Information, Author Contributions, Conflict of Interest, Author Information, Abstract, Keywords, Introduction, Materials & Methods, Results, Conclusions, Figures and Tables with Captions, and other Ethics Statements. For further information, see the Journal Instructions for Authors.
  • Any style may be used for your references as long as you follow the same formatting guidelines. The name of the author or authors, the title of the journal or book, the title of an article or chapter (if applicable), the year of publication, the volume and issue (if applicable), and the pagination are all necessary. Digital object identifiers, or DOI numbers, are strongly encouraged but not required. It is advised to use the bibliographical software programs EndNote, Zotero, Mendeley, or Reference Manager.
  • When your manuscript is ready for editing, you will be asked to style it in accordance with the journal's requirements.

Cover Letter

Every time a manuscript is submitted, a cover letter must accompany it. You can attach the entire version of your manuscript to the cover letter, and it should include the full names and affiliations of the authors as well as additional information regarding identifying text that has been deleted from the article. It should also be brief and provide an explanation of the significance of the paper's content, setting the findings within the framework of previous research and demonstrating how it aligns with the journal's aim. Verify that neither the paper nor any of its contents are pending review or publication in another journal at this time. It is necessary to acknowledge any previous submissions of the work to Nature Lens: The Forensic Science journals. Instead of in the cover letter, the identities of the suggested and disqualified reviewers should be listed in the submission system.

Author Identification

It is recommended that authors include a 300–1500 character biography with their submission and submit it to SciProfiles. The following points should be included in a single paragraph:

  • Complete names of the authors, followed by their present jobs.
  • Educational history, providing details on the school and the year of graduation (the kind of degree earned).
  • Experience in the workplace.
  • Interests in both past and present research.
  • Professional society memberships and honors obtained.
  • The final version of the published work will include an icon pointing to your online ORCID profile if your manuscript is accepted for publication.

Author Affiliation

The affiliation where the majority of the research for the book was conducted should be listed by each author, along with their current affiliation. We advise listing the affiliation where the majority of the research was carried out or funded as the primary, but please confirm with your institution if there are any requirements for a contractual arrangement.

It is crucial that author affiliations and identities are accurate. A lack of proper attribution or an erroneous citation might result from inaccurate information, and it may even cause issues with funding or marketing. It may not be possible to amend or correct the author's affiliation or address after the article has been published.

Independent Researcher

The authors should identify themselves as "Independent Researchers" if none of them are currently or were linked with a university, organization, or business at the time the publication was being developed.

Bibliographic Information

The heading of the reference list should be "References," and it should contain only published or forthcoming references cited by number in the text. Published abstracts (duly noted as being published in a Proceedings), printed manufacturers' protocols or instructions, and internet documents may be validly cited as references. Personal communications and submitted manuscripts are not valid references. Personal communications should be cited in the text, in parentheses, at the appropriate location. The References header should be bolded. Number references consecutively in the order in which they are first mentioned in the text. Identify references in tables and legends by Arabic numerals. References cited only in tables or legends should be numbered in accordance with a sequence established by the first identification in the text of the particular table or figure. Within the text, tables and figures, cite references by Arabic numeral in square brackets [ ]. Within the Reference list, number the references 1., 2., 3., etc. References in the Reference list should be cited in Vancouver style, in accordance with ICMJE Recommendations.
